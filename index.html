Background & Objective

Tuberculosis (TB) remains a critical public health challenge in tribal regions of Madhya Pradesh. Doctors For You NGO conducts active case-finding surveys across tribal communities in Mandla District through multiple rounds of door-to-door screening.

This project analyzes 375 confirmed TB-positive patients from 3 survey rounds to identify risk patterns, predict positivity likelihood, and provide evidence-based recommendations to strengthen tribal TB programs.

Gap Identified

🔍
Gap 1: No Predictive Tool

No data-driven risk scoring system exists to prioritize high-risk individuals before symptom onset.

🔍
Gap 2: Inconsistent Data Entry

Missing Nikshay IDs, inconsistent Yes/No formats, and #N/A entries reduce data reliability for decision-making.

🔍
Gap 3: Low Symptom Documentation

Only 54.9% cough, 7.2% fever reported among positives — classical TB symptoms are under-reported in tribal patients.

Recommendations

💡
Risk Scoring Model

Build a simple 5-variable TB Risk Score: Age (46–60), Tribal status, Alcohol use, Cough, and X-Ray result to triage high-risk patients faster.

💡
Standardize Data Entry

Implement dropdown menus in survey forms to eliminate Yes/No inconsistencies and mandatory Nikshay ID fields.

💡
Asymptomatic Screening

Since 45% of positives had no cough, mandate X-ray for all tribal adults aged 30+ regardless of symptoms.

💡
Block-level Targeting

Focus intensified campaigns on Mandla (105) and Bichhiya (62) blocks showing highest TB burden.

💡
Follow-up System

Create SMS-based alert system for 19 ongoing patients to prevent lost-to-follow-up and reduce 2.4% mortality.

Conclusion

Tribal communities in Mandla district bear a disproportionate TB burden (74.1%). Active case-finding across 3 rounds identified 375 confirmed positives with 74.7% treatment success. A data-driven TB risk scoring model can significantly improve early identification and treatment initiation, particularly for asymptomatic tribal patients aged 46–60 years who constitute the highest-risk demographic.

"Data is not just numbers — for tribal TB patients, it is the difference between early treatment and preventable death."
